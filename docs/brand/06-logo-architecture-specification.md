# 06. Logo Architecture Specification

## Purpose

Define the approved logo architecture for the Diabetes Universe ecosystem:
objectives, design principles, mandatory requirements, evaluation and rejection
criteria, approved concept directions, development and evaluation workflows,
scalability, digital, animation, and accessibility requirements, brand consistency
rules, and prohibited directions.

This document is the authoritative logo architecture specification. It governs
how the logo symbol is conceived and evaluated without creating the final logo,
SVG assets, app icon designs, or derivative brand materials.

## Status

Architecture Approved

## Objectives

- establish a symbol that is recognizable without text across all approved product
  surfaces;
- define mandatory technical and strategic requirements for logo concepts;
- approve exactly three architecture directions for concept development;
- provide explicit evaluation and rejection criteria before visual execution;
- ensure the logo scales from favicon to outdoor advertising without redesign;
- align logo architecture with
  [05 Brand Architecture Specification](05-brand-architecture-specification.md)
  without duplicating strategic brand content;
- prevent premature selection of a winning concept or production of final assets
  in this lifecycle stage.

## Design Principles

Logo architecture follows the visual philosophy and distinctive asset principles
defined in [05 Brand Architecture Specification](05-brand-architecture-specification.md):

- modern and calm;
- precise and minimalist;
- technologically credible without futuristic noise;
- human without childishness;
- medically responsible without clinical clichés;
- simple, cohesive, confident forms;
- scalable and suitable for motion;
- recognizable through form, composition, rhythm, and space—not decoration.

## Mandatory Logo Requirements

The logo must:

- be recognizable without text;
- work at 16×16 pixels;
- work as an App Icon;
- work as a favicon;
- work in monochrome;
- work without color;
- scale from favicon to outdoor advertising;
- animate easily;
- not depend on a wordmark;
- contain no fine details;
- remain modern for at least ten years.

## Evaluation Criteria

Each concept is evaluated against:

- recognizability;
- originality;
- distinctiveness;
- scalability;
- digital applicability;
- print suitability;
- animation suitability;
- international universality;
- alignment with Brand Architecture.

Failure to meet any mandatory requirement or evaluation criterion results in
concept rejection.

## Rejection Criteria

The following are prohibited:

- medical symbols;
- clip art;
- decorative details;
- random geometry;
- letter monogram without an independent idea;
- short-term design trends;
- decorative gradients;
- visual similarity to competitors.

See also [Prohibited Directions](#prohibited-directions) and
[05 Brand Architecture Specification — Prohibited Brand Directions](05-brand-architecture-specification.md#prohibited-brand-directions).

## Logo Architecture Directions

Only three directions are approved for concept development:

1. **Concept A — Universe**
2. **Concept B — Orbit**
3. **Concept C — DU Symbol**

No additional directions may be introduced without separate architectural approval.
No winning concept may be selected in this document.

## Concept A — Universe

### Associations

- ecosystem;
- orbits;
- movement;
- scale;
- interconnection.

Concept A expresses Diabetes Universe as a connected ecosystem of products,
services, and daily actions without literal medical symbolism.

## Concept B — Orbit

Minimalist orbital architecture.

### Associations

- balance;
- continuity;
- life cycle;
- digital ecosystem.

Concept B expresses stable, continuous support through a simple orbital form suitable
for small digital sizes and motion.

## Concept C — DU Symbol

A fully original symbol.

- Do not use a direct D/U letter monogram.
- The symbol must become an independent brand mark recognizable without the
  word **Diabetes Universe**.

Concept C must pass the same mandatory requirements and evaluation criteria as
Concepts A and B.

## Concept Development Workflow

The mandatory sequence for each approved direction is:

1. **Idea analysis** — validate strategic fit with Brand Architecture and logo
   objectives.
2. **Symbol architecture** — define form logic, proportions, and structural rules
   before sketching.
3. **Sketches** — explore variations within the approved direction only.
4. **Selection** — shortlist concepts that meet mandatory requirements.
5. **Refinement** — improve chosen directions without adding prohibited elements.
6. **Final mark** — produce the approved symbol definition for the direction.
7. **Derivative brand elements** — define how the mark extends to approved
   derivative assets in later documents.

Skipping a step or introducing a new direction without approval is not permitted.

## Concept Evaluation Workflow

After each concept direction is developed, an audit is mandatory.

1. Verify all [Mandatory Logo Requirements](#mandatory-logo-requirements).
2. Score against every item in [Evaluation Criteria](#evaluation-criteria).
3. Apply [Rejection Criteria](#rejection-criteria) and
   [Prohibited Directions](#prohibited-directions).
4. Confirm alignment with
   [05 Brand Architecture Specification](05-brand-architecture-specification.md).
5. Record pass, fail, or revision required before any concept advances.

A concept that fails any mandatory criterion is rejected and must not proceed
without a new approved revision cycle.

## Scalability Requirements

The logo architecture must support:

- favicon and 16×16 UI contexts;
- app icon grids on iOS and Android;
- navigation marks and toolbar glyphs;
- marketing layouts from digital banners to outdoor formats;
- monochrome and single-color reproduction;
- clear silhouette recognition at minimum size.

Forms must remain legible without reliance on color, gradient, or fine interior
detail.

## Digital Requirements

The logo must be suitable for:

- raster and vector digital delivery;
- light and dark interface backgrounds;
- high-DPI and standard-density screens;
- product UI, documentation, marketing site, and API/developer surfaces;
- consistent rendering across web, mobile, and desktop without redesign.

Digital execution details belong to later approved brand documents. This section
defines architectural requirements only.

## Animation Requirements

The logo architecture must allow:

- simple, purposeful motion without decorative excess;
- loop-friendly forms for loading, onboarding, and brand moments;
- clear readability during and after animation;
- performance-safe animation on mobile and web.

Animation must not depend on color transitions or complex interior detail to remain
recognizable.

## Accessibility Requirements

Logo architecture must not degrade:

- recognition at small sizes for users with low vision;
- contrast when placed on approved light or dark backgrounds;
- understanding of brand identity independent of color alone;
- clarity when reproduced in monochrome accessibility modes.

Aesthetics never take priority over recognizability, contrast, or safety-related
communication surfaces defined in Brand Architecture.

## Brand Consistency Rules

- The logo symbol must remain consistent with immutable brand decisions in
  [05 Brand Architecture Specification](05-brand-architecture-specification.md).
- The symbol must not introduce medical, clinical, or competitor-adjacent
  semantics prohibited by brand architecture.
- The symbol must function independently of wordmark, tagline, or UI chrome.
- Approved concept directions must not be merged or hybridized without
  architectural approval.
- Derivative uses must preserve proportion, clear space, and minimum size rules
  defined in later documents.

## Prohibited Directions

In addition to [Rejection Criteria](#rejection-criteria), the logo must not use:

- blood drop;
- syringe;
- medical cross;
- heart;
- pancreas;
- DNA;
- pill;
- stethoscope;
- medical case;
- shield;
- typical health-app symbols;
- direct D/U letter monogram;
- clip art;
- random geometry;
- decorative gradients without functional reason;
- short-term visual trends.

## Success Criteria

Logo architecture is successful when:

- mandatory requirements, evaluation criteria, and rejection criteria are
  documented and enforceable;
- exactly three concept directions are approved without selecting a winner;
- development and evaluation workflows are explicit and sequential;
- scalability, digital, animation, accessibility, and consistency rules align
  with Brand Architecture;
- documentation navigation reflects the logo architecture entry;
- no final logo, SVG, image assets, or app icon designs are produced in this
  stage;
- contradictions with approved architecture are reported as **Blocked** instead
  of being resolved through independent logo redesign.
